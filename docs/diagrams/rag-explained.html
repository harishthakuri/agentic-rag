RAG, explained

Retrieval-Augmented Generation: how an AI answers from your documents instead of from memory.

Without RAG: the AI answers from memory
“How many vacation days do new employees get?”
“Typically 20 days.”
What the AI actually knows
What it learned in trainingPublic web pages, books and code, up to a fixed cut-off date.
Your company's documentsHandbook, policies, contracts, internal notes: never seen.
  • It has never read your handbook, so it guesses, confidently.
  • It can't show where the answer came from.
  • Its knowledge stops at the date it was trained.
With RAG: the AI looks it up first
R
RetrieveSearch your documents for passages about the question.
Employee Handbook › Leave › “New employees receive 15 days in their first year.”
A
AugmentHand the AI the question plus the passages found, with the instruction: use only these.
G
GenerateThe AI writes the answer and cites its source.
“New employees get 15 days in their first year 1.”
Sources: 1 Employee Handbook › Leave › New employees
  • Answers come from your own, current documents.
  • Every claim links to its source, so anyone can check it.
  • If the documents don't say, it answers “I couldn't find this”.

Think of an open-book exam. The AI is a bright student who must answer by looking things up in the book you gave it (your documents) and citing the page, rather than relying on what it half-remembers. Add a new document, and the “book” is up to date immediately. No retraining needed.

The building blocks

How documents are prepared so the right passage can be found in a fraction of a second.

1 · Chunking
Rules defined in our code, not AI

Each document is cut into passages of about half a page, at headings and paragraphs, never mid-sentence.

Why: the AI should read the 3–5 relevant passages, not a 200-page manual, and each citation can point to the exact spot.

→
2 · Embedding
Done by AI: the embedding model

An embedding model (AI) reads each passage and produces its meaning fingerprint: a list of numbers.

[0.021, −0.134, 0.087, 0.310, …, 0.045]1,024 numbers per passage

Why: passages that mean the same thing get similar numbers, even when they use different words.

→
3 · Vector

That list of numbers is a vector: a point on a “map of meaning”. Close points mean similar content.

Why: “which passages answer this?” becomes a simple distance check that computers do very fast.

→
4 · Vector database
Done by the database (PostgreSQL)

Stores every passage with its vector, plus a word index for exact terms such as names and codes.

Why: a special index finds the nearest vectors among millions without checking each one. We use PostgreSQL with pgvector.

The map of meaning, and how search uses it

The question gets a fingerprint too and lands on the map. The closest passages are the most relevant, here “Cache busting”, even though the question never uses those words. Simplified to 2 dimensions; real fingerprints have 1,024.

How many AI models does it take?

Simple RAG and our Agentic RAG need the same two AI models. The difference is what the chat model is asked to do, and how often.

1 · Embedding model “the translator”

Turns text into meaning fingerprints. Used for every passage at upload and for every question. It is small and fast (about 0.2 s per question).

qwen3-embedding:8b · runs on our machine
2 · Chat model “the reader and writer”

Reads and writes language. In simple RAG it only writes answers. In Agentic RAG it also reviews the passages found and plans which searches to run. This is the larger, slower model.

Uploads use only the embedding model. The chat model is needed only when someone asks a question.

Optional third model (on our roadmap): a small, dedicated “reviewer” model could replace the chat model in the review step. Same job, about 0.2 s instead of about 4 s.

Why not one model for everything? Each model does a different job. Embedding models compare meaning quickly, covering thousands of passages per minute; chat models read and write. Searching with a chat model would be far too slow, and an embedding model cannot write an answer.

What “×1–6” means

The search step can repeat up to 6 times, a safety limit so the AI can't loop forever. Reading around a passage or listing the documents also counts. At the limit, the tools are taken away and the AI must answer with what it has. In our evaluation the agent needed 1.3 searches per question on average.

Which one should I use?

Direct question with clear words (“What is the default ef_search?”)Ask
Two or more parts, or a comparison (“Compare HNSW and IVFFlat”)Agent
Unsure which words the documents useAgent
Fastest, most predictable resultAsk
